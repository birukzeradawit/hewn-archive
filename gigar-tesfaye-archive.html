Gigar Tesfaye

Archaeologist, Historian, Researcher, and epigraphist

Gigar Tesfaye Woldemichael (1934–2007 E.C.) was an Ethiopian archaeologist, historian, and heritage researcher who devoted his life to documenting and studying Ethiopia’s archaeological, historical, and cultural heritage.

His early education was rooted in Ethiopian traditional scholarship, including the study of Ge’ez and historical texts. He later pursued studies in Paris, France, including Oriental languages and ancient Christian art. This combination of Ethiopian and European scholarly traditions supported his lifelong research into Ethiopia’s history and heritage.

Throughout his career, he conducted field research across Tigray, Wollo, and other parts of Ethiopia, documenting archaeological sites, churches, monasteries, ancient inscriptions, rock art, manuscripts, and historical evidence. His research included important sites and subjects such as Lalibela, Aksum, Gundagundi, Wali Eyesus, Meket, King Kaleb, and ancient inscriptions in Ge’ez and Sabaean.

Gigar Tesfaye combined field observation, photography, historical research, and written documentation to record Ethiopia’s heritage. His research was published in scholarly journals, including Annales d’Éthiopie and the Journal of Ethiopian Studies, as well as in Ethiopian newspapers.

He also participated in archaeological research beyond Ethiopia, including fieldwork in southern Yemen in 1972 with French researchers.

Gigar Tesfaye was known for his dedication, humility, honesty, and tireless commitment to research. He continued working with his research materials and historical documentation throughout his life, including during his final years, with the aim of preserving and sharing his findings.

His Legacy

Gigar Tesfaye left behind a valuable body of research notes, photographs, field documentation, publications, and historical records. His work provides an important resource for understanding Ethiopia’s archaeology, history, inscriptions, religious heritage, and cultural landscapes.

The Gigar Tesfaye Archive Project seeks to preserve, organize, document, and make this legacy accessible to researchers, heritage professionals, students, and future generations.

“His work continues to speak through the evidence he carefully documented and preserved.”
