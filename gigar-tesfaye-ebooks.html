01 1985
Annales d’Éthiopie 1985 · Vol. 13

La généalogie des trois tribus Irob chrétiennes d'après des documents de Gunda Gundié

Author: Gigar Tesfaye

A pioneering genealogical and historical monograph reconstructing the lineage, oral traditions, and territorial settlements of the three Christian Saho-Irob tribes residing in the Tigray highlands (Hasaballa, Buknaito, and Adgaddi). Discovered in historical manuscripts preserved at the isolated monastic center of Gunda Gundié, this study decodes both the legendary traditions tracing descent from King David to Warada-Mehrat, and the verified historical lineage of Hasaballa through the Seyums of Agamé. Includes comprehensive genealogical trees, clan divisions, and historical geographical notes.

02 2000
Annales d’Éthiopie 2000 · Vol. 16

Rock Art around the Zalanbesa Area in the Eastern Zone of Tigrai (Ethiopia)

Author: Gigar Tesfay

A critical archaeological field report conducted for the Centre for Research and Conservation of Cultural Heritage (CRCCH) documenting prehistoric rock art discovered across Eastern Tigrai near Zalanbesa. Details three major prehistoric sites: bovine cave paintings along the Misohil river in Anza, white paintings depicting seven lions at Babuda Miblae Anbesa, and prehistoric engravings of cattle discovered on flat stone tablets at Mai Gona, documenting northern Ethiopian pastoralist traditions and rock art iconography.

Annales d’Éthiopie 1987 · Vol. 14

Bä12ñaw mäto kefelä zämän negus Lalibäla rasu bä'asäraw mänbärä tabotoč say täqärṣäw yätägäñu yäge'ez ṣehufoč

Author: Gigar Tesfaye

The foundational primary Amharic monograph publishing the 1972–1975 research discoveries in Lasta (Wollo), deciphering ancient 12th-century Ge’ez royal inscriptions carved directly into the sacred wooden altars (mänbärä tabot) made by King Lalibela. Documents inscriptions in Biet Gabriel-Lalibela, Biet Mikael-Golgotha, Biet Medhane Alem, and Biet Giyorgis, providing authentic epigraphical evidence of the historical Lalibela monarchy, liturgical dedications, and royal prayers dedicated to Christ and the Virgin Mary.

05 1982
Journal of Ethiopian Studies August 1982 · Vol. 15

Les deux inscriptions du négus Kaleb en Arabie du Sud

Authors: Jacqueline Pirenne & Gigar Tesfaye

An extensive epigraphical and historical study of the two major royal Ge’ez inscriptions on white marble commissioned by King Kaleb (Elesbaas) following his victorious 525 AD expedition to South Arabia to protect the Christian communities of Najran and Himyar. Based on field study during the French Archaeological Mission to North Yemen, this work provides complete Ge’ez transcriptions, French translations, and commentaries on the fragments discovered in Mârib and Zafar.

Citation: Journal of Ethiopian Studies, Vol. 15, August 1982, pp. 105–122 Pages: 105–122 (18 pages) Publisher: Institute of Ethiopian Studies Format: Ge’ez Inscriptions & Facsimiles

06 1974
Journal of Ethiopian Studies July 1974 · Vol. 12, No. 2

Reconnaissance de Trois Eglises Antérieures a 1314

Author: Gigar Tesfaye

A pioneering archaeological and historical reconnaissance investigating three very ancient Tigrayan religious centers founded before 1314: the monastery of Maryam Sawné (fief of the children of Sebuha, with foundations predating Emperor Wedem Ar’ed, 1300–1314), the ancient church of Saint Qirqos (carved stone capitals and Eusebian miniature canons), and the cliff-hanging rupestral sanctuary of Maryam Dongolate (Zagwe-era silver processional cross, ancient painted canvases, and a rare 16th-century European enamel triptych with Ge’ez inscriptions).

Citation: Journal of Ethiopian Studies, Vol. 12, No. 2, July 1974, pp. 57–75 Pages: 57–75 (19 pages + 20 Photographic Plates) Publisher: Institute of Ethiopian Studies Focus Sites: Maryam Sawné, Saint Qirqos, Maryam Dongolate

07 1984
Journal of Ethiopian Studies November 1984 · Vol. 17

Inscriptions sur bois de trois églises de Lalibala

Authors: Gigar Tesfaye (avec la collaboration de Jacqueline Pirenne)

An exhaustive epigraphical and paleographical study examining fourteen ancient inscribed wooden objects preserved in the rock-hewn sanctuaries of Lalibela (Biet Gabriel, Biet Mikael, Biet Mädhané Aläm, and Biet Giyorgis). Analyzes four inscribed wooden panels portraying the Transfiguration on Mount Tabor and ten sacred wooden altars (mänbärä tabot) dedicated by King Lalibela, including the extraordinary 56-line text of Golgotha 1 dedicated to the Sunday Sabbath, with complete Ge’ez transcriptions and French translations.

Citation: Journal of Ethiopian Studies, Vol. 17, November 1984, pp. 107–126 Pages: 107–126 (20 pages + 18 Photographic Plates) Publisher: Institute of Ethiopian Studies Focus Objects: 4 Transfiguration Panels · 10 Mänbärä Tabot

08 1979
Abbay · CNRS Paris 1979 · Cahier No. 10

Études archéologiques et historiques dans le Tigray — Gunda Gundie et couvents stéphanites

Author: Gigar Tesfaye · Publication intégrée en deux volets (Abbay, Cahier n° 10)

Two complementary research monographs published together in Abbay: Documents pour servir à l'histoire de la civilisation éthiopienne (Cahier n° 10, Editions du CNRS, Paris, 1979), combining prehistoric rock art reconnaissance and monastic archival investigations in the Agamé region of Eastern Tigray:

PART 1 · ITEM 8 [ PP. 75–78 ]

Découverte de gravures préhistoriques dans la vallée de Gunda Gundie (Agame, Tigray)

Field discovery and archaeological recording of ancient pecked rock engravings along the sandstone canyons and river cliffs of the Gunda Gundie valley (Agamé, Eastern Tigray). Documents representations of wild fauna, long-horned cattle, and hunting motifs reflecting ancient pastoral traditions.

PART 2 · ITEM 9 [ PP. 93–100 ]

Notes sur Gunda Gunde et d'autres couvents stephanites dans le Tigray

Comprehensive historical and monastic investigation into the sanctuary of Gunda Gunde (Dabra Gärzen) and remote Tigrayan monastic establishments affiliated with the 15th-century Stéphanite (Estifanosite) reform movement. Analyzes monastic defense architecture, ancient Ge’ez manuscripts, epigraphy, and the historical survival of Stéphanite community heritage.

Citation: Abbay, Cahier No. 10, 1979, pp. 75–78 & pp. 93–100 Pages: pp. 75–78 & 93–100 (12 pages total) Publisher: Éditions du CNRS (Paris) Series: Abbay · Fascicule 10
